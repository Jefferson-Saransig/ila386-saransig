Can you additionally add vertical dashed lines to indicate where 68% and 95% of the data lie? (Ignore any warning messages about binwidth that may arise).

Exercise 3 Imagine reading a research paper about a grammaticality rating study. It is noted that the mean acceptability rating for a particular grammatical construction is 5.25 with a standard deviation of 0.4. Assuming normality, what is the interval within which you expect 68% of the data to lie? What about 95% of the data? Do you think the assumption of approximate normality is reasonable in this case?

M <- 5.25
SD <- 0.4

lower_68 <- M - SD
upper_68 <- M + SD

lower_68
## [1] 4.85
upper_68
## [1] 5.65

95%

lower_95 <- M - 2 * SD
upper_95 <- M + 2 * SD

lower_95
## [1] 4.45
upper_95
## [1] 6.05

problem: Likert-scale data is discrete (only whole numbers are possible responses), while the normal distribution is a continuous model (mismatch) “While the calculated 68% and 95% intervals numerically fit within a plausible Likert scale (e.g., 1–7), there are still reasons to question the assumption of approximate normality. First, Likert-scale responses are discrete rather than continuous, which conflicts with the continuous nature that a normal distribution assumes. Second, since the mean (5.25) sits closer to the upper end of a typical scale, there is less room for values to spread out above the mean than below it, which could produce a skewed rather than symmetric distribution — undermining the normality assumption even though we cannot confirm this from the summary statistics alone.”
